# Security

## Reporting a problem

If you think you've found a security problem in Stackapella, in this code or on stackapella.com,
please tell us privately: **contact@stackapella.com** (the address
[security.txt](https://stackapella.com/.well-known/security.txt) names), or GitHub's private
vulnerability reporting on this repository. Please don't open a public issue.

What helps:

- what you found, and where (the page, the request or the file);
- how to see it happen, step by step;
- what someone could do with it.

## What we ask

- Test only with your own account, or test accounts you made, and never look at other people's
  recordings, messages or details.
- Don't change or delete anything that isn't yours, and don't slow the service for others (no
  load testing or denial of service against stackapella.com; run your own install for that).
- Give us a reasonable time to fix it before telling anyone else.

## What we promise

- An answer within 3 working days, and news until it's fixed.
- No action against anyone who reports in good faith and follows the above.
- With your OK, thanks by name once it's fixed. There's no bug bounty.

## In scope

- The code in this repository, and the apps built from it.
- The service at stackapella.com and its subdomains.

Out of scope: a self-hosted install run by someone else (tell its operator), reports from
automated scanners without a way to reproduce them, missing best-practice headers with no
demonstrated effect, and social engineering of our team or users.

The longer version is the [security page](https://stackapella.com/legal/security). For how to
disclose responsibly in general, see
[OWASP's vulnerability disclosure cheat sheet](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Vulnerability_Disclosure_Cheat_Sheet.html).
