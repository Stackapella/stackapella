## 1. What it teaches, in one line

Hear how far apart two notes are, whether a chord is major or minor, and where home is, and **sing
it back**: ears and voice trained together, which is what a harmony singer needs. Every lesson ends
in singing; listening rounds teach, sung answers give the stars.

- **Course id** `ear`, lesson prefix `ear`, colour 6, picture `echo`.
- **4 units, 16 lessons, about 3 to 4.5 minutes each.**
- **Home first:** every echo and every listening round starts with a short I chord (or do-so-do)
  in its key, because solfège only means something inside a key.
- Leaps are named in plain words first ("a leap of three", "a small three"), solfège beside them,
  the textbook name small ("minor third"). "Me" (the minor third) is said "may".
- **Every sung note sits a fourth below to a fifth above the learner's note (do).** Each upward
  leap has its allowed starting notes, mirrored for leaps down:

| Leap up | Starts from |
|---|---|
| a step (half or whole) | so below up to mi |
| three (small or big) | so below up to re |
| four | so below, do or re |
| five | so below or do |
| six | so below or la below |
| an octave | so below only (to so above) |

## 2. What the app measures (and what it doesn't)

| Step | What it measures | Stars |
|---|---|---|
| **choose** (the lead's) | Which clip or word you tap, first answer only | Never the lesson's stars: every lesson ends in a sung step. Listening teaches and is shown ("5 of 6 heard right") |
| **bullseye** (mine) | Where each sung note landed (`landing`) | Landing, as in Perfect your pitch |
| **bullseye, `named`** (new) | The same, but a note nearer another semitone than the target is a miss, not a weak landing, and Hear yourself names what you sang ("In tune, but a leap of four. Twinkle's is one step wider.") | Landing, a miss counting nothing |

A call can differ from its answer (built: the guide sings one note, you sing the leap from it; the
guide sings a phrase, you sing home; a chord sounds, you sing its root).

**Not measured:** whether you sang the right syllable, or how fast you answered. The content doc
planned reaction time; it's dropped (no timers: an untimed answer is fairer to a beginner).

**About ears:** true tone deafness is rare (about 1 to 4 percent of people); the course page says
most people who think they can't hear it just haven't trained ear and voice together.

## 3. The lessons

Each lesson runs **Why, Hear, Try, Do, Hear yourself**. After a sung answer, **Hear it right**
plays the guide singing the leap you were asked for, beside your take. After two weak takes a step
offers Sing it with the guide (when the lead's engine has it).

**Your leaps** (the memory of the course): each leap has one song, met in its lesson and kept on a
small card ("a leap of four: Amazing Grace"), tap to hear.

**Then and now**: every challenge opens with the same six-leap round from Song starts, compared
with your first ("Then: 4 of 6 found. Now: 6 of 6.").

### Unit 1: Leaps you already know

| Lesson | The skill | Why, Hear, Try, Do | Stars from |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ear-1-songs: Song starts** (the free taste) | Hear a leap through a tune you know, and sing it | Why: you already carry these leaps in songs you know. Hear the first four notes of Oh When the Saints (do-mi, a leap of three), Amazing Grace (so below to do, four) and Twinkle, Twinkle (do-so, five), then each leap on its own. Try: echo each leap in the guide's key. Do, round 1: sing the three openings in the guide's key (an easy win). Round 2: the same leaps from a new note the guide gives. Hear yourself: the darts, "You found 5 of 6 leaps", then **your leap stacked over the guide's note**: "That's harmony: two notes a leap of three apart." | landing, named and generous (two stars for a fair try) |
| **ear-1-updown: Up or down, step or leap** | Direction and size | Choose: "Up or down?", "Which is bigger?" (two leaps compared), "A step or a leap?" Try and Do: echo do-re-mi-fa-so and back, with numbers, then three-note shapes (do-mi-so, so-mi-do) | landing |
| **ear-1-threefive: Three or five?** | Tell a leap of three from a leap of five | Hear Saints beside Twinkle. Choose 6 rounds, a third of them the earlier leaps. Do: sing each from new notes | landing, named |
| **ear-1-challenge: Name that leap** | Three, four and five, up | Then and now; choose 6 rounds; then the guide gives a note and the screen names a leap ("up a leap of four"), you sing it, four times | landing, named |

### Unit 2: More leaps

| Lesson | The skill | Why, Hear, Try, Do | Stars from |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ear-2-fourfive: Four or five?** | The hardest beginner pair | Hear Amazing Grace's four beside Twinkle's five (a five is a 3 to 2 ratio of frequencies). Choose 6. Do: sing each from new notes | landing, named |
| **ear-2-steps: Small and smaller** | The half step and the whole step | Hear Ode to Joy's opening (mi-mi-fa-so: a half step, then a whole step, up) beside Frère Jacques (do-re). Choose 6. Do: sing each up and down | landing, named |
| **ear-2-thirds: Big and small threes** | The major and minor third | Hear Saints (do-mi) beside Greensleeves (la below to do: a small three, and a minor tune). Choose 6. Do: sing each from new notes | landing, named |
| **ear-2-wide: Six and eight** | The sixth and the octave | Hear My Bonnie (so below to mi, six), and the octave as "the same note, higher" (it vibrates exactly twice as fast). Choose 6 rounds of five, six or eight. Do: sing them from so below | landing, named |
| **ear-2-down: Leaps down** | The same leaps falling | Hear Swing Low (mi-do, a big three down), This Old Man (so-mi, a small three down), O Come, All Ye Faithful (do-do-so below, four down) and Twinkle backwards (so-do, five down). Choose 6. Do: four falling leaps | landing, named |
| **ear-2-challenge: Leap ladder** | Any leap, up or down | Then and now; choose 6; then four leaps the screen names, from notes the guide gives | landing, named |

### Unit 3: Major and minor

| Lesson | The skill | Why, Hear, Try, Do | Stars from |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ear-3-hear: Bright or dark** | Hear a major or minor chord | Why: the chord's middle note, the third, decides it. Hear a major chord, then its minor. Choose 8: "Major or minor?" (chords, then broken chords). Do: echo the broken chords, do-mi-so then do-me-so | landing |
| **ear-3-third: Sing the third** | Make a chord major or minor | The guide sings do and so; you sing mi between them, then me. Self-check: "Did you hear it change colour?" | landing, named |
| **ear-3-tunes: Major and minor tunes** | Hear it in a tune | Choose 6: Frère Jacques and Twinkle each in major and minor, "Which one is minor?" Do: Frère Jacques' first line in minor | landing |
| **ear-3-challenge: Fill the middle** | The third of each chord in a phrase | Then and now. Try: the guide sings each chord's root and fifth and then its third. Do: the guide sings root and fifth, you fill in the middle note, through I, IV and V (the third of IV sung as la below, of V as ti below) | landing, named |

### Unit 4: Find home

| Lesson | The skill | Why, Hear, Try, Do | Stars from |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ear-4-home: Where's home?** | Feel home after a phrase | Why: most tunes want to end at home. Choose 6: "Did it end at home?" Do: after each phrase sing home; the key moves each time (do anywhere from so below to re), and home is fine in whichever octave fits | landing, named |
| **ear-4-root: The chord's root** | Sing the note a chord is built on | Hear and Try: the chord broken from the bottom, then together. Do: the chord together only, you sing its root (I's do, IV's fa above, V's so below). Your brain can hear a root that isn't even played (the missing fundamental) | landing, named |
| **ear-4-challenge: Sing the bass** | Roots under a tune | Then and now; the guide sings Amazing Grace's first two lines over their chords; at each change you sing its root, as a bass would. Hear yourself stacks your roots under the tune: "You just sang bass." | landing, named |

## 4. The fun

- **Your leaps**: the card that fills with each leap's song. Later (after launch): a small ring
  per leap that fills when it's heard right first time in three separate sittings.
- **Bullseye darts** for every sung answer, and **your leap stacked over the guide** ("That's
  harmony").
- **A run inside choose**: "6 in a row" (never a penalty for a miss; the why always follows, and a
  missed pair comes back later in the lesson, unscored, so you end on a win).
- **Mystery song** at each unit's end: the guide sings two notes of a tune you haven't met (O
  Christmas Tree, so below to do); you name the leap, then the tune is revealed and joins your card.
- **"You just did that"**: your first leap from a note you'd never heard it on, in the inner ring
  (lesson 1 reliably earns it); six rounds in a row; your first root sung after hearing only the
  chord; each unit's challenge.
- **Then and now** at every challenge (the same six-leap round).
- Facts on end screens where they fit: 3 to 2 on Four or five, 2 to 1 on Six and eight, the missing
  fundamental on The chord's root.

## 5. Safe singing

Short, easy notes at a medium volume in the middle of your voice. Big leaps start lower so the top
stays easy. No hold over 4 seconds. The course page has the usual line: stop if anything
tightens or hurts; don't practise when hoarse or ill; no app replaces a teacher.

## 7. Songs and their status

Only the first few notes of each, on "loo" or the tune's first word, in our own setting. All
public domain in the US and the EU: Oh When the Saints (spiritual), Amazing Grace (Newton died
1807; "New Britain", 1829), Twinkle, Twinkle (French tune, 1761; Jane Taylor died 1824), Ode to
Joy (Beethoven, 1824), Frère Jacques (traditional), Greensleeves (anonymous, 16th century), My
Bonnie Lies over the Ocean (traditional; the 1881 arranger died 1898), Swing Low, Sweet Chariot
(spiritual, published 1872), This Old Man (traditional), O Come, All Ye Faithful (18th-century
tune), O Christmas Tree (traditional). Take Me Out to the Ball Game is left out: public domain in
the US, but not in the EU until 2027 (Von Tilzer died 1956) and 2030 (Norworth died 1959).

## 8. What the reviews changed

**Aural-skills teacher:** Take Me Out to the Ball Game dropped (not public domain in the EU), the
octave taught as "the same note, higher"; Für Elise (a half step down, and a piano piece) replaced
by Ode to Joy's opening, which gives a half step and a whole step up in one tune; every leap's
starting notes limited to the span; the thirds of IV and V sung as la and ti below, IV's root as fa
above and V's as so below; two-answer rounds never give the stars (guessing would pass); a note
nearer another semitone counts as a miss and is named; home set before every round; a scale echo
and "Which is bigger?" rounds in unit 1; Find home's key moves each time; The chord's root plays
the chord together in Do (a broken chord from the bottom gives the root away); leaps down
widened to four and five; Greensleeves for the small three (Brahms' leap comes after a repeated
note); Hear it right after each answer; the reaction-time measure dropped and logged; lengths
capped (the challenges at 6 rounds and 4 sung).

**Game designer:** Song starts made a hook: the tune's opening, an easy round in the guide's key,
then a new note, generous stars, and your leap stacked over the guide's note ("That's harmony");
then and now from this course's own lesson 1; confusing two leaps reads as "a leap of four", never
as "way off"; four-or-five moved to unit 2 and Three or five? in its place, each leap with one
song; Bright or dark ends in singing; Fill the middle (the guide sings root and fifth, you sing the
third) with a Try first; earlier leaps in a third of later rounds; Your leaps as the memory system;
Unit 2 renamed More leaps; "6 in a row"; the moments made reachable; Mystery song; a missed pair
replayed unscored; Sing the bass ends in "You just sang bass."

## 9. Reading the course file

The lessons are `web/course/plus/ear/course.json` (see `web/course/plus/README.md`), in the free
course's shape. Lesson ids never change once landed. **Every `step` is in semitones from the
learner's comfortable note** (0 is do, 7 is so, -3 is la below); solfège in the words is the
learner's.

- `choose` (the lead's): listening rounds. A clip's `chords` give the key first; `options` are words
  to tap; `answer` indexes the options, or the clips when there are none.
- `bullseye` (`web/js/components/bullseye.js`, fields in `docs/courses/perfect-pitch.md`, section 9).
  Ear training uses `named: true` on every step that asks for a leap: the guide sings the first
  note (`call`), you sing only the leap (`sing`), and the note you sing must be that leap (not the
  same note an octave off, not the next semitone), else it's a miss and Hear yourself names the
  leap you sang. A group's `key` sets its home chord (`home: true`), and `label` says what to sing.
- The rules every lesson keeps (the span, holds under 4 seconds, stars only from singing, each leap
  up only from its allowed starting notes, every round's answer a real choice) are checked by
  `web/tests/ear.test.js`.

## 10. What the lead's quality review changed

- A named leap is sung on its own (it had been the guide's note and then the leap, so half the
  scored notes were an echo), an unsung leap is a miss, and a leap is judged in its own octave.
- Fill the middle's scored step has no chord under it (its third was sounding).
- Labels in the free lesson's rounds ("Saints: up a leap of three"), and the challenges' first round
  called what it is: the round from your first lesson, again (a true then and now waits for the
  lead's keep and then-and-now).
- Fours and fives come from more than so-do and do-so (re to so, la below to mi, ti below to mi, so
  below to re), so "did it land home?" can't answer them.
- The chord's root asks in a new order; Sing the bass follows the hymn's chords phrase by phrase.
- Sing the third gains a sung try; the measures lines no longer claim a weighting they don't have.
- Not yet: Mystery song, the "6 in a row" run and the Your leaps card (after launch).

**The lead's last look** (same day): the challenges' first round is called "Your first round, again"
(no then-and-now card yet), the listening rounds' answers are mixed so their order gives nothing
away, and the step texts say exactly what is sung.
