# The free course

Eight units, 32 lessons, from finding your comfortable note to recording all four parts of Amazing
Grace and shaping Danny Boy. Its lesson-by-lesson review is
[course-review-plan.md](../course-review-plan.md); this page keeps its facts and their sources. The
course file is `course/course.json` in the courses repo, laid out by hand: edit its text in place,
since `tools/coursefmt.py` would reflow the whole file.

Unit 1's first three lessons are left as they are while the onboarding experiment (onb1) enrols.

## Facts, and where they're told

Each is its lesson's `fact`, the lesson end's "Did you know?" card, with its source. None is told in another course. Ut queant laxis is Folk's, Mahler's Frère Jacques is Ear
training's and Sumer is icumen in is Counterpoint's. The free course keeps Beethoven's deafness and
Amazing Grace's 1773 sermon: everyone sees it first, so another course repeating one of them changes its own.

| Lesson | Fact | Sources |
|---|---|---|
| 1-challenge | Your vocal folds open and close once for every wave of a note: about 110 times a second in a typical man's speaking voice, about 210 in a woman's. | Titze, *Principles of Voice Production*, 1994; Baken and Orlikoff, *Clinical Measurement of Speech and Voice*, 2000 (averages vary by study) |
| 3-steps-up | Across the world's music, melodies move mostly by small steps; leaps are far rarer. | Vos and Troost, Music Perception 6 (1989) 383-96; Savage, Brown, Sakai and Currie, PNAS 112 (2015) 8987-92 |
| 3-challenge | Beethoven was almost totally deaf when his Ninth Symphony was first played (Vienna, 7 May 1824); its tune is the European Union's anthem. | Britannica, "Symphony No. 9 in D Minor"; Council of Europe, "The European Anthem" (coe.int): Council of Europe 1972, the EU 1985 |
| 4-challenge | John Newton, a slave-ship captain who became a clergyman, wrote Amazing Grace for his New Year's Day sermon in 1773; words and tune met in print in 1835. | Cowper and Newton Museum, "Amazing Grace" (Newton's diary, 1 January 1773); Hymnology Archive, "Amazing grace!": first set to "New Britain" in Walker's *Southern Harmony*, 1835 |
| 5-round | Are You Sleeping is the French round Frère Jacques; a manuscript of canons from the 1700s, in France's national library, credits it to Jean-Philippe Rameau, though whether he wrote it is still debated. | Wikipedia, "Frère Jacques", a pointer to the Bibliothèque nationale's manuscripts (the Decroix collection of 86 canons); IMSLP, "Frère Jacques (Rameau)", which lists a c. 1772 copy (F-Pn MS-1843). Sumer is icumen in, checked first, is Counterpoint's |
| 6-tenor | "Tenor" comes from the Latin for "to hold": in medieval music the tenor held the main tune, often in long notes. | Britannica, "Tenor"; Grove, "Tenor" |
| 7-challenge | In 1951 Les Paul layered take after take of Mary Ford's voice on one tape for "How High the Moon", nine weeks a US number one. | Sound on Sound, "Classic Tracks: Les Paul and Mary Ford, How High The Moon"; Whitburn's Billboard chart books (sound-on-sound overdubbing on one machine, not yet multitrack) |
| 8-challenge | The tune was printed in 1855 as the Londonderry Air; Frederic Weatherly set his "Danny Boy" words to it in 1913. | Petrie, *The Petrie Collection of the Ancient Music of Ireland*, Dublin 1855; Dictionary of Ulster Biography, "Jane Ross" (she collected it; from whom is undocumented) |

Unit 2's fact is its drone lesson's headline: "Singers have tuned to drones for centuries, from Indian
classical music to choir warm-ups". Beating (the wobble against a drone) is Perfect your pitch's fact,
so the free course says only what you hear: the wobble slows as you get closer and stops when you match.

## What the 2026-10-29 review changed

- One or two facts a unit, from the list above, each checked against its sources.
- A safety line where the free course first holds a note (1-breath) and where it climbs
  (8-high-vowels); do named as the home note (6-tenor); 7-blend's cards teach blending, not the studio.
- A record step's closing line now shows after its recording is checked (it never showed before, so
  8-challenge's line was hidden).
