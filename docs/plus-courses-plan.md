# Plus courses: what else we can teach a voice to do

Status: brainstorm (Hillel, 2026-10-28). The free course teaches harmony from a first note. Plus
courses are deeper, separate courses for people who want more, each a few units long and built on
the same lessons, lanes and scoring. Counterpoint ([course-review-plan.md](course-review-plan.md))
and vibrato ([vibrato-plan.md](vibrato-plan.md)) come first.

How to read the list: **Ready** means our pitch, timing and range tools already measure what it
teaches. **Needs** names the new listening it would take. Every course still needs lessons,
words and real-voice examples written and checked.

## Singing skills

| Course | What you learn | Can we score it? |
|---|---|---|
| **Vibrato** | Straight tone, finding the wave, letting it happen, choosing it | Ready (rate, width, evenness: see the vibrato plan) |
| **Counterpoint** | Drones, rounds, descants, species counterpoint | Ready, plus partner lines (the course review) |
| **Range and registers** | Chest, head voice, falsetto and the mix, smooth breaks, safe high and low notes | Ready for range and pitch; register needs timbre (later) |
| **Belting, safely** | A strong, bright sound without strain | Needs loudness and timbre; health notes and caution |
| **Breath and stamina** | Long phrases, support, quiet starts | Ready (note length, steadiness, quiet onsets) |
| **Diction for singers** | Pure vowels, consonants on time, Latin and Italian for choir | Needs vowel recognition (formants) |
| **Sight-singing** | Read a stave and sing it, with solfège | Ready (the Staff view plus pitch scoring) |
| **Ear training** | Intervals, chords, major and minor, by ear | Ready (listen and choose, the course review's M5) |
| **Perfect your pitch** | Hit a named note cold, hold it, tune to a drone | Ready |

## Styles

| Course | What you learn | Can we score it? |
|---|---|---|
| **Scat singing** | Jazz syllables, swing, landing on chord tones, short improvised lines | Ready for pitch, rhythm and chord tones; swing feel needs timing analysis |
| **Barbershop and tags** | Ringing chords, just intonation, the classic tags | Ready, plus pure tuning targets (the course review's M6) |
| **Gospel and soul runs** | Riffs, runs, blue notes | Ready for pitch (fast note runs) |
| **Yodeling** | Quick flips between chest and head voice | Ready for the pitch leaps; the flip's timbre later |
| **Folk and early music** | Straight tone, modes, drones | Ready |
| **Musical theatre** | Acting a song, dynamics, belt and legato | Partly (loudness, phrasing) |
| **Pop and country styling** | Slides, cries, twang, breathy tone | Pitch slides ready; tone colour later |
| **A cappella bass** | Low lines, "dm dm" bass syllables, holding a groove | Ready for pitch and timing |
| **Singing in another language** | Hebrew, Spanish, French, Latin songs, sounds first | Needs diction scoring for the words |

## Beyond singing

| Course | What you learn | Can we score it? |
|---|---|---|
| **Whistling** | In tune, long notes, melodies, vibrato on a whistle | Ready: a whistle is a clean pitch, easy to track |
| **Beatboxing** | Kick, snare, hi-hat, simple grooves, then over a song | Needs percussive sound recognition and onset timing |
| **Vocal percussion for a cappella** | The drum part under a group | Same as beatboxing |
| **Humming and lip trills** | Warm-ups that build tone | Ready |
| **Overtone singing** | Two notes at once, as in Tuvan throat singing | Needs harmonic tracking: advanced, a showpiece |
| **Live looping** | Build a song by stacking parts in real time | Ready (the studio, with a looping mode) |
| **Improvising a harmony** | Hear a tune, invent a part above or below on the spot | Ready (chord tones and voice leading) |
| **Character voices** | Voices for stories and games: a pirate, a robot, a giant, a cartoon squeak | Needs timbre; pitch range ready |
| **Accents for performance** | Sounds of a regional accent for a role or a song | Needs vowel recognition |

**Impressions of famous people:** better as **styles and characters** ("a 1950s crooner", "an opera
tenor", "a country twang") than named people. Naming a real person risks their right of publicity
and trademarks, and the real voice clips we'd need aren't ours to use. A "sound like a crooner"
course gets most of the fun safely.

## How we'd choose what to build

1. Courses our tools already score well first: vibrato, counterpoint, whistling, sight-singing, ear
   training, barbershop tags, scat (pitch and chord tones), a cappella bass, live looping.
2. Then the ones needing one new kind of listening, built once and reused: **timbre and vowels**
   (formants) open diction, accents, registers, character voices and belting; **percussive sounds**
   (onsets and their colour) open beatboxing and vocal percussion.
3. Health first: belting, registers and anything loud or high get careful words, short sessions and
   "stop if it hurts".
