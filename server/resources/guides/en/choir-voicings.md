# SSA, SSAA, TTBB and SAB: choir voicings explained

A choir piece's **voicing** is the list of parts it's written for, printed on the music as
letters: **S** for soprano, **A** for alto, **T** for tenor and **B** for bass (or, in SAB,
baritone). SATB, the four parts of most choir music, is the best known. The others tell you how a
piece splits or leaves out those parts, and so which choirs can sing it.

## Reading the letters

- **One letter, one part.** SATB has four parts: soprano, alto, tenor and bass. If you're new to
  them, start with [what SATB means](/guides/satb-explained).
- **A letter twice means that section splits in two.** SSA has two soprano parts and one alto part.
- **Split parts are numbered from the top.** Soprano 1 sings above Soprano 2, and Tenor 1 above
  Tenor 2.

## Upper voices: SA, SSA and SSAA

- **SA** is two parts, soprano and alto. It's common for children's choirs, and a gentle first step into harmony.
- **SSA** is three parts: first soprano, second soprano and alto.
- **SSAA** is four: two soprano parts and two alto parts.

Music like this is often called **treble** or **upper-voice** music. Women's, girls' and children's
choirs sing it most, but anyone whose voice sits in those ranges can.

## Lower voices: TTB, TBB and TTBB

- **TTBB** is four parts: first tenor, second tenor, baritone and bass (sometimes called first and
  second bass).
- **TTB** is the usual three-part version, with the tenors split. **TBB**, with the lower voices
  split, is rarer.

Men's choirs sing these most. Barbershop also has four parts, but they're named tenor, lead,
baritone and bass, with the tune in the lead, the second part from the top. The [barbershop course](/courses/barbershop) explains how they fit.

## Mixed voices with few men: SAB

**SAB** is soprano, alto and baritone: three parts, with one lower part for all the men, written
where most men's voices can reach it, between tenor and bass. It suits choirs with only a few
tenors and basses. **Three-part mixed** is a close relative, and some publishers use the two names
for the same music. Where they differ, three-part mixed puts the men's part higher, for school
choirs whose boys' voices are just starting to change.

## More parts: SSATB, divisi and double choir

- **SSATB** is five parts, with the sopranos split. Other five- and six-part voicings split
  different sections, as in SATBB.
- **Divisi** means a section splits only for a passage, marked "div." in the music, and sings one
  part again where it says "unis.".
- **SATB/SATB**, or **double choir**, is two four-part choirs singing together, eight parts in all.

## Which voicing fits your choir?

Count your singers by where their voices sit, not by who they are.

- **Cover every part.** Each part needs at least two or three singers who can hold it, so one
  missing singer doesn't leave a hole.
- **Check the lowest part.** If your lowest voices can't reach a bass part's bottom notes, an SAB
  piece may sound better than an SATB one.
- **Fewer parts, more voices on each.** A small choir often sounds fuller in three parts than
  spread thin across four.

Not sure where a voice sits? The free [vocal range test](/tools/vocal-range-test) finds a singer's
lowest and highest notes and the part that fits them.
