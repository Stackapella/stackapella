# Easy Christmas carols for a small choir

A small choir can sing a lovely Christmas concert, or carols at the door, with a few weeks of
practice, as long as the carols suit it. The easiest are the hymn-like ones: every part moves
together, word by word, and each verse uses the same tune. Every carol below is in four parts
(soprano, alto, tenor and bass), with each part free to learn by ear on its own page.

## What makes a carol easy

- **The parts move together.** When everyone sings the same words at the same time, nobody has to
  count through someone else's line.
- **One tune, verse after verse.** Learn the first verse and the rest is new words on the same
  notes.
- **Comfortable ranges.** No part sits very high or very low for long.
- **Short phrases with room to breathe,** so a small group can stay together without a director
  beating time.

## Ten easy carols to start with

1. [Silent Night](/songs/silent-night): slow and gentle, and the best known of all.
2. [Away in a Manger](/songs/away-in-a-manger): short, quiet, and loved by every age.
3. [O Come, All Ye Faithful](/songs/o-come-all-ye-faithful): a strong tune everyone knows, with a
   chorus that builds.
4. [O Little Town of Bethlehem](/songs/o-little-town-of-bethlehem): calm and even, like a hymn.
5. [Once in Royal David's City](/songs/once-in-royal-davids-city): a steady tune, often sung to
   open a carol service.
6. [While Shepherds Watched](/songs/while-shepherds-watched): plain and singable, one of the oldest
   in this list.
7. [The First Noel](/songs/the-first-noel): a long tune, but the same for every verse and chorus.
8. [Hark! The Herald Angels Sing](/songs/hark-the-herald): bright and confident, good to end with.
9. [Joy to the World](/songs/joy-to-the-world): lively, its tune running down the scale.
10. [The Holly and the Ivy](/songs/the-holly-and-the-ivy): a folk carol with a cheerful refrain.

## Three to stretch you

- [Ding Dong Merrily on High](/songs/ding-dong-merrily-on-high): its long "Gloria" runs need breath
  and practice.
- [Angels We Have Heard on High](/songs/angels-we-have-heard-on-high): another long "Gloria", and a
  favourite once you have it.
- [Coventry Carol](/songs/coventry-carol): slow and haunting, with harmonies that need a careful
  ear.

## With only a few singers

- **Four singers can sing four parts.** A small choir can be one singer a part; two a part is
  safer, so nobody sings alone.
- **Put a strong voice on the tune.** The soprano carries the carol; the other parts lean on it.
- **Missing a part?** Men can sing tenor or bass, and an alto who sits low can cover the tenor an
  octave up for a carol or two. Every carol still sounds whole with the tune, the bass and one
  part between.
- **Learn the parts at home first,** so rehearsals are for singing together. Each carol's page has
  its parts to hear alone or with the others; the guide to
  [free SATB learning tracks](/guides/free-satb-learning-tracks) shows how to use them.

## A three-week plan

- **Week 1:** choose four or five carols, give out the parts, and everyone learns their first
  verse at home.
- **Week 2:** sing them together, a verse at a time; fix the places where parts come apart.
- **Week 3:** sing each carol through, start to finish, and decide who gives the starting note
  (a [pitch pipe](/tools/pitch-pipe) helps).

All the carols are on the [Christmas carols](/songs/christmas-carols) page, with a filter for the
part you sing.
