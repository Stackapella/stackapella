# How to find your voice type

Your voice type is a rough label for where your voice sits: high, low or somewhere between. It
helps you pick a part in a choir or a harmony group. Finding yours takes a few minutes and no
special knowledge.

## Comfort, not extremes

The most useful thing to know is your **comfortable range**: the notes you can sing easily, again
and again, without pushing. It isn't the highest squeak or the lowest growl you can manage once.

A part that lives in your comfortable range is a pleasure to sing. You can think about the music,
the words and the other parts. A part that keeps reaching for your edges is tiring, and it's hard
to sing in tune while you're straining.

## Find your lowest and highest comfortable notes

Find a quiet spot, and sing on an easy "ah".

- **Your lowest comfortable note:** start somewhere in the middle and step down a note at a time.
  Stop when the sound gets thin, breathy or gravelly. The last note that still felt clear and easy
  is your lowest comfortable note.
- **Your highest comfortable note:** go back to the middle and step up. Stop when you feel
  yourself reaching, squeezing or getting louder to make it. The last note that still felt easy
  is your highest comfortable note.

Hold each note for a couple of seconds. If you can hold it steadily without effort, it counts.
If you have to push, it doesn't, at least not yet.

## What your range points to

Once you have your two notes, compare them with the usual choir ranges. These are rough, and
**C4** is middle C:

- **Soprano:** roughly C4 to A5.
- **Alto:** roughly G3 to D5.
- **Tenor:** roughly C3 to A4.
- **Bass:** roughly E2 to E4.

Your range will rarely match one of these exactly. Look for the part whose middle sits in the
middle of your range. That's the one you'll be able to sing longest without tiring.

## A guide, not a box

Voice types are a starting point. They aren't a verdict on your voice, and they aren't fixed.

- **Your range can grow.** With easy, regular singing, many people find a few more comfortable
  notes at the top or bottom over time.
- **Your range changes through the day.** Many people find their voice lower and less flexible
  first thing in the morning, and freer once it's warmed up. Check your range when you've been talking or singing
  for a while.
- **You can sit between two types.** Lots of people do. Try both parts on a song you like, and
  pick the one that feels easier for now.
- **Songs differ.** A part that's too high in one key can be perfect a few notes lower. If a song
  feels wrong, the key may be the problem, not your voice.

There's no better or worse voice type. Choirs need every part, and harmony needs every voice.

## Try it in Stackapella

Michael, Row the Boat Ashore is short and easy, so it's quick to try in more than one part.

- Open the song and use **Check my range**. Sing your lowest comfortable note on "ah" and hold
  it, then your highest. The app tells you your comfortable range, marks the part that suits you
  best, and suggests a key that fits your voice.
- Choose that part, **Listen** to it with its guide voice, then **Sing along**. If you're between
  two parts, try both, and notice which one feels easier by the end.
- Set note names to letters (like D4), do re mi, or numbers, whichever you find easiest to follow.
- When it feels comfortable, **Record** your part and stack another over it.
