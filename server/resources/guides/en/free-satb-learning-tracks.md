# Free SATB learning tracks: practise your choir part at home

A learning track is a recording made to help one singer learn one part. Choirs use them so that
rehearsal can go on blend and shape instead of notes. Most are sold per song; here's what they are,
how to use them, and where to hear every part of a song free.

## The three kinds of learning track

- **Part alone:** only your part, so you can hear exactly what you sing.
- **Part-dominant:** the whole song with your part louder, so you hear your line and how it fits.
- **Part-missing:** the whole song with your part left out, so you can sing it into the gap, as you
  will in rehearsal.

Use them in that order: alone to learn the notes, dominant to place them, missing to check you can
hold them.

## How to practise with them

Start a week or so before rehearsal, in short sessions. Listen to your part alone once through with
the words, then sing along with it a phrase at a time. Move to the dominant track when the notes feel
familiar, and to the missing track when you can sing your part through without it. The guide to
[learning your choir part fast](/guides/practise-a-choir-part) goes step by step.

Sing at the written pitch, even when you slow a hard bar down: slowing changes the speed, not the
notes. And record yourself once with the missing track, then listen back. It's the quickest way to
hear where you drift.

## Where to find free ones

Every song in [Stackapella's library](/songs) works as a free set of learning tracks, in the browser,
with no account needed to listen. Each song has a page for each part (the alto page of
[Silent Night](/songs/silent-night/alto), for example) where you can play your part alone or with
the others, and sing along as each note lights up. Choirs use the
[Christmas carols](/songs/christmas-carols) and [hymns](/songs/hymns) most this time of year.

With a free account, the app also makes a practice track for you: export any song with one part
louder, or left out, and take it anywhere.

## Common questions

**What does SATB stand for?** Soprano, alto, tenor and bass, the four parts of most choir music.
The guide to [SATB](/guides/satb-explained) explains each one.

**Do I need to read music?** No. Learning tracks are for learning by ear.

**Are part-dominant tracks better than part-alone ones?** They do different jobs. Alone teaches the
notes; dominant shows how they sit in the chord. Use both.
