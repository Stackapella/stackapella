# How to run a choir sectional: a plan for section leaders

A **sectional** is a rehearsal for one section of a choir on its own: the sopranos, the altos, the
tenors or the basses. Without the other parts sounding, singers can hear their own line, fix notes
and rhythms, and work on blend, so the full rehearsal can be about the music. Directors often
hand sectionals to section leaders, so here's a plan you can follow even if you've never led one.

## Before the sectional

## 1. Agree one goal with the director

Ask the director what they want from it: the notes of the new piece, a page that keeps going
wrong, the tuning in the slow movement. One clear goal does more than a list. Agree the bars, and
write them down.

## 2. Prepare your own part

Know the section's line well enough to sing it confidently and slowly. Whatever you sing as an
example, the section will copy, so a wrong note or a sagging pitch from you becomes everyone's.

## 3. Bring what you need

A copy of the music for everyone, pencils, a way to give notes (a keyboard, or the free
[pitch pipe](/tools/pitch-pipe) on a phone), and a recording or a way to play the other parts if
you have one.

## During the sectional

## 4. Warm up briefly

Five minutes of warming up gets voices and ears working, ideally on something from the music
itself: a phrase from the hard bars, sung on one vowel.

## 5. Start with the hardest place

Go straight to the bars you agreed. Sing them slowly, then a phrase at a time:

- **Notes first,** at half speed, on a vowel or on solfege, before the words.
- **Then the rhythm,** spoken in time on the words.
- **Then together,** notes and words, still slow.
- **Then up to speed,** a little at a time.

## 6. Tune the long notes

Hold the long notes, and any chords where your section divides, and listen. A section sounds like one voice when its vowels
match and its pitch is steady. The free [drone](/tools/drone) gives you a fixed note to tune
against; the guide to [blending in a choir](/guides/how-to-blend-in-a-choir) has more.

## 7. Put it back in context

Once the hard bars work, sing them with the bars before and after, so singers can get in and out
of them. If you can, sing them against the other parts, from a piano, a recording or a song player,
so the section hears where its notes fit in the chord.

## 8. Mark it and end on a high

Ask everyone to pencil in what you fixed: breaths, a note to listen for, a tricky interval. End by
singing the whole passage once at speed, so the section leaves hearing how much better it sounds.

## After the sectional

Tell the director what you fixed and what still needs work, and ask singers to practise their part
at home before the next full rehearsal. The guide to
[practising a choir part](/guides/practise-a-choir-part) is a good one to pass on.

Stackapella gives each singer their part to learn at home: on every song's page, tap a part to
hear it on its own, or hear it with the others; in the app, sing it over the other parts. For a
carol, send your section [Silent Night](/songs/silent-night) and its
[alto part](/songs/silent-night/alto) before you meet, so the sectional can start from the music
