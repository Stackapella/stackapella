# How to record vocal harmonies at home

Recording harmonies on your own is easier than it sounds. You sing one part, then sing the next
while you hear the first, and so on until every part is your voice. You don't need a studio or
music software. You need headphones, a quiet room, a few minutes to set up, and parts you can sing.
This guide is about the recording. To learn the parts first, the guide to
[singing harmony with yourself](/guides/sing-harmony-with-yourself) starts there.

## 1. Wear headphones

While you record a new part, you hear the parts you've already recorded. Through speakers, they'd
leak into the mic and be recorded again, a little late, under your new take. With headphones on,
only your voice reaches the mic.

Wired headphones keep the timing tightest. Bluetooth headphones work too, but they add a delay of
their own, so check the timing with them on (step 3).

## 2. Set up the mic and the room

- **The mic:** the one in your laptop or phone is enough to start. A USB mic sounds fuller and
  picks up less of the room.
- **The distance:** a hand's width or two from the mic, and the same for every take, so the parts
  match. Turn a little away for a loud breath.
- **The room:** quiet, and soft. Curtains, a sofa and a rug soak up echo; a bare kitchen or
  bathroom rings. Turn off fans and anything that hums.
- **The level:** sing your loudest phrase and watch the meter. It should move well but never hit
  the top. The free [mic test](/tools/mic-test) shows your level.

## 3. Check the timing once

Every computer and phone takes a moment to send sound to your headphones and to bring your voice
back from the mic. Left alone, that delay puts each new part a little behind the others, and a
stack of four parts drifts. Measure the delay once and it's taken off every take. Measure it again
whenever you change headphones or mics. The free [audio latency test](/tools/audio-latency-test)
measures it in your browser.

## 4. Record the tune first, then the bass

The tune is the part everyone knows, so it sets the timing and the phrasing for the rest. Record it
first, as well as you can. Then record the bass, the part furthest from the tune, which holds the
chords up. Last, the parts in between, which are easiest to place once the top and bottom are
there.

## 5. Keep every take in time

- **Count in.** A bar of clicks before you sing means every part starts on the same beat.
- **Use a click if the song is steady.** A metronome under the first part keeps it even, and the
  later parts follow it. The free [metronome](/tools/metronome) works in your browser.
- **Fix a phrase, not the whole part.** If one line is late or off pitch, sing just that line
  again rather than the whole song.
- **Trim the ends.** Cut the breath before the first note and the click when you stop, so the
  parts start and end together.

## 6. Mix it

- **Levels:** keep the tune on top and the harmonies a little under it. If you can't hear the tune
  clearly, the others are too loud.
- **Pan:** keep the tune in the middle and spread the harmonies a little left and right. The
  chord sounds wider, and each part is easier to hear.
- **A room:** a touch of reverb makes separate takes sound like one group singing together. Too
  much and the words blur.
- **Fuller:** some singers record the tune twice and pan the two takes apart. The small
  differences between them make it sound bigger.

Listen on headphones and on a phone speaker before you share it: a mix that works on both works
almost everywhere.

## Common questions

**Do I need a DAW or music software?** No. You can record, line up and mix every part in your
browser. A DAW helps later, for detailed editing.

**Do I need a good mic?** Not to start. Headphones and a quiet room make more difference than the
mic. Use the same mic and distance for every part, so they match.

**Why are my parts out of time?** Usually the delay between your headphones and the mic. Check the
timing again, especially after changing headphones. If one part is still off, nudge it, or
record it again with a count-in.

**Why do my harmonies sound out of tune together?** Each part may be in tune with the guide but
not with the others. Listen to the part under yours while you sing, and hold long notes steady
rather than sliding into them. The guide to [singing in tune](/guides/singing-in-tune) helps.
