# What is a cappella? Meaning, styles and how to sing it

A cappella means singing with no instruments: the voices are all the music. A choir singing a hymn
without an organ, four friends singing a barbershop tag, a group singing a pop song with one voice
on the bass line and another on the beat: all of it is a cappella.

## Where the word comes from

"A cappella" is Italian for "in the manner of the chapel". It first meant church music sung by
voices alone, without the instruments heard in other music of the time. Today it means any singing
without accompaniment, sacred or not.

## How a cappella works

With no instruments, the voices do every job:

- **The tune:** one voice or part carries the melody.
- **The harmony:** other parts sing the notes of the chords around it.
- **The bass:** the lowest voice holds the chords up, as a bass guitar or a piano's left hand would.
- **The beat, sometimes:** in modern groups, one singer adds
  [vocal percussion](/guides/vocal-percussion), the drum kit made with the mouth.

That's why a cappella is such good practice for hearing harmony: nothing covers the parts, so every
note you sing is heard against the others.

## The main styles

- **Choral:** choirs singing in parts, often four: soprano, alto, tenor and bass
  ([SATB](/guides/satb-explained)).
- **Barbershop:** four parts in close harmony, with the tune usually in the second voice from the
  top and chords that ring. The guide to [barbershop tags](/guides/barbershop-tags) is a way in.
- **Contemporary:** groups covering pop songs, with a lead singer, backing vocals, a bass line and
  vocal percussion.
- **Folk, gospel and traditional:** songs sung in harmony without instruments for generations,
  from sea shanties to spirituals.

## How to start singing a cappella

1. **Learn one part by ear.** Pick a short song in four parts and learn one of them, the tune or a
   harmony, from a guide voice singing it.
2. **Hear it with the others.** Sing your part while the other parts play, so you learn to hold it.
3. **Sing it alone, or with friends.** Find a few singers, or record each part yourself, one over
   the other; the guide to [singing harmony with yourself](/guides/sing-harmony-with-yourself)
   shows how.

Every song in [Stackapella's library](/songs) is an a cappella arrangement, each part free to learn
by ear. [Amazing Grace](/songs/amazing-grace) is a good first one.

## Common questions

**Is a cappella always harmony?** No: one voice singing alone is a cappella too. But most a cappella
music is in parts.

**Do you need to read music to sing a cappella?** No. Many a cappella singers learn their parts by
ear.

**How many singers make an a cappella group?** As few as three or four. Many groups have five to
twelve, and choirs many more.
