# Copyright policy

Stackapella lets people record harmonies and share them by link. We respect copyright, and we
expect everyone who shares on Stackapella to do the same. The songs in our library are in the
public domain, our arrangements are under CC BY-SA 4.0, and our guide recordings are licensed to
you to sing with and share inside your harmonies (see the terms). A harmony you share must be your own singing or playing.

## Reporting a shared harmony or a template

Every shared page, and every template in the library, has a **Report** button, and you don't need
an account to use it. Choose "Copyright" and tell us briefly why. Reports from several people hide
a page, or take a template out of the library, until we have looked at it.

## Sending a formal copyright notice (DMCA)

If you believe a shared harmony, a template, or a song made from imported sheet music infringes your
copyright, send a notice to our designated agent.

:dmca_agent

Your notice must include:

1. your physical or electronic signature;
2. the copyrighted work you believe is infringed;
3. the link to the shared harmony (`https://stackapella.com/h/...`), or the template in the
   library, or (for a song shared within a choir group) its name and the group;
4. your name, address, telephone number and email address;
5. a statement that you believe in good faith that the use isn't authorised by you, your agent or
   the law;
6. a statement, under penalty of perjury, that the notice is accurate and that you are the owner,
   or authorised to act for the owner.

When we receive a complete notice, we take the page (or the template) down at once and tell the
person who shared it, with a copy of the notice (your contact details are removed unless the law
requires them). Projects people already made from a template keep their copy.

## Songs made from imported sheet music

Someone may make a song from sheet music they bring (a file they have, read on their own device),
after confirming they have the right to use it; we keep when they confirmed, and which wording they
agreed to. Such a song stays private to them and the choir groups they share it with: it is never
public, never in the library or the gallery, never offered to other singers as an idea, and nothing
sung from it can be shared by link. A member of the group can report it with the Report button.
When we take one down after a notice, it's gone for the group, and its owner is told why and how to
send a counter-notice.

## Counter-notice

If your harmony was taken down and you believe that was a mistake (for example, it's your own
singing of a public domain song), you may send a counter-notice to the same agent with:

1. your physical or electronic signature;
2. the link to the harmony that was taken down;
3. a statement, under penalty of perjury, that you believe in good faith it was removed by mistake
   or misidentification;
4. your name, address and telephone number, and a statement that you consent to the jurisdiction
   of the federal court for your address (or, outside the United States, any judicial district in
   which Stackapella may be found), and that you will accept service of process from the person
   who sent the notice.

We pass the counter-notice to the person who sent the notice. Unless they tell us within 10 to 14
business days that they have filed a court action, we may restore the page.

## Repeat infringers

An account whose shared harmonies, templates or imported songs are taken down for copyright three
times may lose sharing, or be closed, and can no longer make songs from imported sheet music. Each
takedown is recorded, and we look at the account before acting.

## In the European Union and the United Kingdom

Anyone may report content they believe is illegal with the Report button, without an account. We
review every report, and when we take something down we tell the person who shared it why, and how
to ask us to look again.
